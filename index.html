Overall Conclusion

  • Nearly all ESRD haemodialysis patients (98.8%) exhibited chronically elevated hs-Troponin I far exceeding the standard 99th-percentile diagnostic threshold of 14 ng/L, confirming that the standard ACS cut-off is not applicable in this population.
  • Older age was the sole independent predictor reaching statistical significance (B = +0.016, p = 0.003), suggesting that myocardial stress accumulates over time in ESRD even after adjusting for other risk factors.
  • Dialysis adequacy (Kt/V) showed a consistent inverse relationship with troponin — lower Kt/V = higher troponin — significant in univariate analysis (rs = −0.34, p = 0.001) and borderline in the multivariable model (p = 0.051), indicating inadequate dialysis may contribute to myocardial stress.
  • Elevated ultrafiltration volume and a history of cerebrovascular accident were significantly more common in the high-troponin group, highlighting the systemic cardiovascular burden in these patients.
  • ECG changes (ST depression, T-wave inversion, LVH, QTc prolongation) were widespread across all patients but did not differ significantly between high- and low-troponin groups, underscoring that troponin elevation is independent of surface ECG morphology in ESRD.
  • QRS duration was the only ECG parameter significantly different between groups (p = 0.046), though this likely reflects ventricular conduction variability rather than a direct troponin-driven process.

Data Point Explanation

Tap any row in the table to see a detailed explanation of that data point and its clinical significance.

Age

The high-troponin group was significantly older (median 67 vs. 58 years). Age is one of the most consistent drivers of troponin elevation in ESRD — cumulative exposure to uraemic toxins, myocardial fibrosis, and LV remodelling over decades leads to progressive cardiomyocyte damage and release.

p = 0.033 — Significant ✓
Male Sex

Males showed a trend toward higher troponin (63.4% vs 42.9%), but this did not reach significance. The trend is biologically plausible — males may have greater LV mass and different myocardial remodelling responses. However, with n=83 this study was underpowered to confirm a sex effect.

p = 0.076 — Not significant
Body Mass Index (BMI)

BMI was virtually identical between groups (27.3 vs 27.6 kg/m²), indicating that body composition and obesity do not independently drive troponin elevation in this cohort. Both groups were in the overweight range, consistent with the haemodialysis population.

p = 0.880 — Not significant
Smoking Status

Smoking distribution was nearly identical (~78% non-smokers in both). Smoking history does not contribute meaningfully to chronic troponin elevation in established ESRD, likely because uraemic and haemodynamic mechanisms dominate.

p = 0.777 — Not significant
Dialysis Duration

Dialysis duration was almost identical (~51.7 months both). The p = 0.050 falls exactly at threshold and was pre-specified as non-significant. Spearman correlation (rs = +0.12, p = 0.279) also confirmed no linear relationship.

p = 0.050 — Not significant (borderline)
Kt/V (Dialysis Adequacy)

As a median-split, Kt/V did not differ significantly (1.13 vs 1.25, p = 0.849). However, as a continuous variable: Spearman rs = −0.34 (p = 0.001) and GLM (p = 0.051) confirm a clinically meaningful inverse relationship: a 0.1-unit drop in Kt/V ≈ 6.5% rise in troponin.

p = 0.849 as dichotomous — misleading; see Table 3
Ultrafiltration Volume

The high-troponin group had significantly higher UF volumes (2615 vs 2577 mL, p = 0.040). Larger UF reflects greater fluid overload between sessions — haemodynamic stress (volume overload → wall stress → myocardial stretch) may drive troponin release through myocardial strain, even without acute ischaemia.

p = 0.040 — Significant ✓
Vascular Access

No significant difference in vascular access type. AV fistulas were most common (~83%) in both groups. Access type does not appear to influence troponin levels in this cohort.

p = 0.517 — Not significant
Diabetes Mellitus

Diabetes present in ~50% overall, slightly higher in the high group (53.7% vs 45.2%), not statistically significant. In the multivariable GLM, diabetes showed a borderline trend (B = +0.301, p = 0.052), suggesting a possible independent contribution the study was underpowered to confirm.

p = 0.443 — Not significant
Hypertension

Hypertension prevalence was identical between groups (68.3% vs 69.0%). Despite being a major driver of LVH, it does not discriminate high from low troponin groups — likely because it is near-universal in the ESRD population.

p = 0.941 — Not significant
Ischaemic Heart Disease

IHD was more prevalent in the high-troponin group (31.7% vs 19.0%) — biologically plausible — but did not reach significance, likely due to small sample size.

p = 0.185 — Not significant
Cerebrovascular Accident (Stroke)

A history of stroke was strikingly more common in the high-troponin group (14.6% vs 2.4%, p = 0.045). This reflects shared pathophysiology: both troponin elevation and stroke in ESRD are driven by uraemic cardiomyopathy, endothelial dysfunction, and systemic atherosclerosis.

p = 0.045 — Significant ✓
Heart Failure

Heart failure prevalence was identical between groups (7.3% vs 7.1%). With only 6 cases total, there was insufficient power to detect a difference. Heart failure in ESRD is often subclinical or underdiagnosed.

p = 0.976 — Not significant
hs-Troponin I (Defining Variable)

Groups defined by the cohort median of 61.8 ng/L — over 4× the standard URL of 14 ng/L. Critically, 98.8% of ALL patients exceeded 14 ng/L, demonstrating that the standard ACS cut-off is clinically meaningless in haemodialysis patients.

Groups defined by this cut-off — by design
C-Reactive Protein (CRP)

CRP did not differ between groups (8.9 vs 8.0 mg/L, p = 0.178). Spearman correlation also showed no relationship (rs = +0.06, p = 0.593). Systemic inflammation alone is not the primary mechanism — structural and haemodynamic mechanisms dominate.

p = 0.178 — Not significant

Sinus Rhythm

80.5% of the high-troponin group maintained sinus rhythm vs 90.5% in the low group, but this was not significant (p = 0.379). The higher rate of non-sinus rhythm in the high group is driven largely by AF/flutter clustering — consistent with elevated troponin reflecting greater atrial and ventricular remodelling.

p = 0.379 — Not significant
Atrial Fibrillation / Flutter

AF/flutter was more than twice as prevalent in the high-troponin group (17.1% vs 7.1%), though no formal p-value was calculated due to small expected counts. In ESRD, AF is associated with volume overload, electrolyte disturbances, and structural atrial disease — all of which also promote troponin elevation.

No p-value (small counts)
Paced Rhythm

Only 2 patients (one per group) had paced rhythms — far too few to draw conclusions. Paced rhythm also confounds ECG interpretation, so these patients' ECG morphology data should be interpreted cautiously.

n = 2 — Insufficient for analysis
QTc Interval

Despite the theoretical concern that higher troponin might accompany longer QTc, there was no significant difference between groups (430 vs 445 ms, p = 0.593). Wide IQRs in the high group suggest electrolyte fluctuations inherent to haemodialysis drive variability.

p = 0.593 — Not significant
QRS Duration ★ Significant

The only ECG interval to differ significantly (94 ms high vs 100 ms low, p = 0.046). Paradoxically, the high-troponin group had a shorter median QRS. Wide IQRs (62–126 ms in high group) suggest high within-group variability — likely a subset with intraventricular conduction defects skewing the IQR rather than a true biological effect.

p = 0.046 — Significant ✓ (interpret cautiously)
PQ Interval

Available in 72/83 patients (AF and paced rhythm patients excluded). No significant difference between groups (172 vs 163 ms, p = 0.641). PR interval prolongation suggesting AV nodal disease does not track with troponin levels in this cohort.

p = 0.641 — Not significant
ST-Segment Depression (STD)

Present in ~29% of all patients — a high background prevalence reflecting uraemic cardiomyopathy. STD was equally distributed between groups (26.8% vs 31.0%, p = 0.679), confirming that chronic troponin elevation in ESRD does not result from ongoing ischaemia detectable by surface ECG.

p = 0.679 — Not significant
T-Wave Inversion (TWI)

T-wave inversion present in 43.4% of all patients — strikingly high, reflecting widespread ventricular strain in ESRD. TWI did not differ between troponin groups (46.3% vs 40.5%, p = 0.590), suggesting it is a marker of the ESRD substrate generally, not specifically linked to troponin magnitude.

p = 0.590 — Not significant
LV Strain Pattern

Defined as simultaneous STD + TWI in lateral leads, LV strain was present in 25.3% overall. Prevalence was nearly identical between troponin groups (24.4% vs 26.2%, p = 0.850) — reinforcing that chronic troponin elevation in ESRD reflects sustained structural damage, not acute ischaemia.

p = 0.850 — Not significant
Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH)

LVH present in 47% of all patients by ECG criteria — consistent with high ESRD prevalence from pressure overload. LVH was not more prevalent in the high-troponin group (43.9% vs 50.0%, p = 0.578), suggesting LVH per se is not the primary mechanism of chronic troponin elevation.

p = 0.578 — Not significant
Pathological Q Waves

Q waves more common in high-troponin group (12.2% vs 4.8%) but not significantly so (p = 0.265). The multivariable GLM confirmed no significant independent contribution (B = −0.385, p = 0.146), even after adjusting for age, Kt/V, and diabetes.

p = 0.265 — Not significant
Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB)

LBBB was rare (3 patients total) and equally uncommon in both groups. Too few cases to draw conclusions. LBBB can cause troponin elevation through mechanical dyssynchrony independent of coronary disease.

p = 0.830 — Not significant (n too small)
QTc Prolongation (>440 ms)

QTc prolongation affected 28.9% of all patients — a high, clinically concerning prevalence. However, prevalence was virtually identical between groups (29.3% vs 28.6%, p = 0.944), confirming that chronically elevated troponin and QTc prolongation are independent manifestations of uraemic cardiomyopathy.

p = 0.944 — Not significant

Age — rs = +0.27, p = 0.015

A weak but statistically significant positive correlation: as age increases, so does hs-Troponin I. Consistent with cumulative myocardial injury in ESRD — decades of uraemic toxin exposure, repetitive haemodynamic stress, and progressive myocardial fibrosis. The effect persisted independently in the multivariable GLM (p = 0.003), confirming age as the primary driver.

p = 0.015 — Significant ✓ | Confirmed in GLM
Kt/V — rs = −0.34, p = 0.001

The strongest correlation: patients with lower dialysis adequacy have higher troponin. rs = −0.34 represents a moderate-strength inverse relationship. Inadequate dialysis leads to uraemic toxin accumulation, directly damaging cardiomyocytes. A 0.1-unit decrease in Kt/V ≈ 6.5% rise in troponin (e^0.0635 = 1.065). Attenuated to borderline in the GLM (p = 0.051) after adjusting for age and diabetes.

p = 0.001 — Strongest correlation ✓
Dialysis Duration — rs = +0.12, p = 0.279

Despite the biological rationale (longer ESRD → more cumulative damage → higher troponin), dialysis duration shows no significant correlation. The rate of myocardial injury may matter more than total duration, or survivor bias flattens the relationship over time.

p = 0.279 — Not significant
CRP — rs = +0.06, p = 0.593

CRP, a marker of systemic inflammation, showed essentially zero correlation (rs = 0.06 is negligible). This argues against systemic inflammation being the primary mechanism — structural, haemodynamic, and uraemic mechanisms dominate instead.

p = 0.593 — Not significant
QRS Duration — rs = −0.22, p = 0.049

A weak inverse correlation that barely crossed the significance threshold. Paradoxically, higher troponin is associated with slightly shorter QRS duration. Likely reflects competing effects from LBBB and paced rhythm in some high-troponin patients. Interpret cautiously — borderline as continuous and non-significant as dichotomous (Table 2).

p = 0.049 — Borderline (interpret with caution)
QTc Interval — rs = +0.06, p = 0.593

No meaningful correlation between QTc and troponin (rs = 0.06 is negligible). In ESRD, QTc prolongation is driven predominantly by electrolyte disturbances (hyperkalaemia, hypocalcaemia) rather than by the degree of troponin-associated myocardial injury.

p = 0.593 — Not significant

Age — B = +0.016, p = 0.003

Age is the only variable reaching independent statistical significance. B = +0.016 means for each additional year of age, log(troponin) increases by 0.016 units — approximately a 1.6% increase in troponin per year of age (e^0.016 ≈ 1.016). The 95% CI fully excludes zero (+0.005 to +0.027), confirming a robust independent effect. Cumulative myocardial injury from ESRD drives troponin elevation progressively with age, independent of other clinical factors.

p = 0.003 — Sole significant independent predictor ✓
Kt/V — B = −0.635, p = 0.051

Kt/V narrowly missed significance after adjusting for age and diabetes (p = 0.051). The 95% CI just touches zero (−1.273 to −0.002). A one-unit increase in Kt/V would correspond to a 47% decrease in troponin (e^−0.635), but a more realistic 0.1-unit increase corresponds to ~6.3% decrease. Attenuation from univariate (p = 0.001) to adjusted (p = 0.051) is likely due to age confounding. With a larger sample, Kt/V would likely achieve independent significance.

p = 0.051 — Borderline; clinically important
Diabetes Mellitus — B = +0.301, p = 0.052

Diabetes mellitus showed a borderline independent association with higher troponin (B = +0.301, p = 0.052). The CI just touches zero (−0.003 to +0.604). B = +0.301 translates to a 35% higher troponin in diabetic vs non-diabetic patients (e^0.301 ≈ 1.35), adjusting for age, Kt/V, and Q waves. Diabetes in ESRD causes additional microvascular myocardial damage, autonomic neuropathy, and accelerated atherosclerosis. Borderline p-value likely reflects insufficient power (n=82, only 41 diabetics).

p = 0.052 — Borderline; likely significant with larger N
Pathological Q Waves — B = −0.385, p = 0.146

Q waves showed no independent association with troponin (p = 0.146). The effect estimate is counterintuitively negative (B = −0.385) — likely a spurious finding from the small number of Q wave cases (n=7). Sensitivity analyses replacing Q waves with LV strain, ST depression, or T-wave inversion all confirmed no significant independent ECG effects — surface ECG morphology does not independently predict troponin magnitude in ESRD.

p = 0.146 — Not significant; ECG not an independent predictor
