Overall Conclusion

  • Nearly all ESRD haemodialysis patients (98.8%) exhibited chronically elevated hs-Troponin I far exceeding the standard 99th-percentile diagnostic threshold of 14 ng/L, confirming that the standard ACS cut-off is not applicable in this population.
  • Older age was the sole independent predictor reaching statistical significance (B = +0.016, p = 0.003), suggesting that myocardial stress accumulates over time in ESRD even after adjusting for other risk factors.
  • Dialysis adequacy (Kt/V) showed a consistent inverse relationship with troponin — lower Kt/V = higher troponin — reaching significance in univariate analysis (rs = −0.34, p = 0.001) and borderline significance in the multivariable model (p = 0.051), indicating inadequate dialysis may contribute to myocardial stress.
  • Elevated ultrafiltration volume and a history of cerebrovascular accident were also significantly more common in the high-troponin group, highlighting the systemic burden of cardiovascular disease in these patients.
  • ECG changes (ST depression, T-wave inversion, LVH, QTc prolongation) were widespread across all patients but did not differ significantly between high- and low-troponin groups, underscoring that troponin elevation is independent of surface ECG morphology in ESRD.
  • QRS duration was the only ECG parameter significantly different between groups (p = 0.046), though this likely reflects ventricular conduction variability rather than a direct troponin-driven process.

Age

The high-troponin group was significantly older (median 67 vs. 58 years). Age is one of the most consistent drivers of troponin elevation in ESRD — cumulative exposure to uraemic toxins, myocardial fibrosis, and LV remodelling over decades of kidney disease leads to progressive cardiomyocyte damage and release.

p = 0.033 — Significant ✓
Male Sex

Males showed a trend toward higher troponin (63.4% vs 42.9%), but this did not reach statistical significance. The trend is biologically plausible — males may have greater LV mass and different myocardial remodelling responses to ESRD. However, with n=83 this study was underpowered to confirm a sex effect.

p = 0.076 — Not significant
Body Mass Index (BMI)

BMI was virtually identical between groups (27.3 vs 27.6 kg/m²), indicating that body composition and obesity do not independently drive troponin elevation in this cohort. Both groups were in the overweight range, consistent with the general haemodialysis population.

p = 0.880 — Not significant
Smoking Status

Smoking distribution was nearly identical between groups (~78% non-smokers in both). This null finding suggests that smoking history does not contribute meaningfully to chronic troponin elevation in established ESRD, possibly because other mechanisms (uraemia, haemodynamic stress) dominate.

p = 0.777 — Not significant
Dialysis Duration

Dialysis duration was almost identical between groups (~51.7 months both). The p-value of 0.050 falls exactly at the threshold and was pre-specified as non-significant. Although longer dialysis theoretically means more cumulative exposure, the Spearman correlation (rs = +0.12, p = 0.279) also confirmed no linear relationship.

p = 0.050 — Not significant (borderline)
Kt/V (Dialysis Adequacy)

As a median-split comparison, Kt/V did not differ significantly (1.13 vs 1.25, p = 0.849). However, this is misleading — median dichotomisation loses statistical power. As a continuous variable in Spearman correlation (rs = −0.34, p = 0.001) and the multivariable GLM (p = 0.051), Kt/V shows a clinically meaningful inverse relationship: a 0.1-unit drop in Kt/V ≈ 6.5% rise in troponin.

p = 0.849 as dichotomous — misleading; see Table 3
Ultrafiltration Volume

The high-troponin group had significantly higher UF volumes (2615 vs 2577 mL, p = 0.040). Larger UF volumes reflect greater fluid overload between dialysis sessions — this haemodynamic stress (volume overload → wall stress → myocardial stretch) may drive troponin release through myocardial strain, even in the absence of acute ischaemia.

p = 0.040 — Significant ✓
Vascular Access

No significant difference in vascular access type between groups. AV fistulas were most common (~83% overall) in both groups, which is expected in a well-established haemodialysis cohort. Access type does not appear to influence troponin levels.

p = 0.517 — Not significant
Diabetes Mellitus

Diabetes was present in ~50% overall, with slightly higher prevalence in the high group (53.7% vs 45.2%), but not statistically significant. Notably, in the multivariable GLM, diabetes showed a borderline trend (B = +0.301, p = 0.052), suggesting a possible independent contribution that this study was underpowered to confirm.

p = 0.443 — Not significant
Hypertension

Hypertension prevalence was identical between groups (68.3% vs 69.0%). Despite hypertension being a major driver of LVH and myocardial disease in ESRD, it does not independently discriminate high from low troponin groups in this cohort, likely because it is near-universal in the population.

p = 0.941 — Not significant
Ischaemic Heart Disease

IHD was more prevalent in the high-troponin group (31.7% vs 19.0%) — a biologically plausible finding since prior myocardial injury leads to ongoing cardiomyocyte damage — but the difference did not reach significance, likely due to small sample size.

p = 0.185 — Not significant
Cerebrovascular Accident (Stroke)

A history of stroke was strikingly more common in the high-troponin group (14.6% vs 2.4%, p = 0.045). This likely reflects shared pathophysiology: both troponin elevation and stroke in ESRD are driven by the same substrate of uraemic cardiomyopathy, endothelial dysfunction, and systemic atherosclerosis — not that one causes the other.

p = 0.045 — Significant ✓
Heart Failure

Heart failure prevalence was identical between groups (7.3% vs 7.1%). This null finding is counterintuitive, but may reflect that heart failure in ESRD is often subclinical or underdiagnosed. Additionally, with only 6 cases total, there was insufficient power to detect a difference.

p = 0.976 — Not significant
hs-Troponin I (Defining Variable)

The groups were defined by the cohort median of 61.8 ng/L — over 4× the standard diagnostic URL of 14 ng/L. Critically, 98.8% of ALL patients exceeded 14 ng/L, meaning nearly every patient in a standard clinical context would have a "positive" troponin — demonstrating that the standard ACS cut-off is clinically meaningless in haemodialysis patients.

Groups defined by this cut-off — by design
C-Reactive Protein (CRP)

CRP, a marker of systemic inflammation, did not differ between groups (8.9 vs 8.0 mg/L, p = 0.178). Spearman correlation also showed no linear relationship (rs = +0.06, p = 0.593). This suggests that acute or chronic systemic inflammation alone is not the primary mechanism driving troponin elevation in this population — structural and haemodynamic mechanisms are more likely.

p = 0.178 — Not significant

Sinus Rhythm

80.5% of the high-troponin group maintained sinus rhythm vs 90.5% in the low group, but this was not significant (p = 0.379). The higher rate of non-sinus rhythm in the high group is driven largely by AF/flutter clustering in that group — a pattern consistent with elevated troponin reflecting greater atrial and ventricular remodelling.

p = 0.379 — Not significant
Atrial Fibrillation / Flutter

AF/flutter was more than twice as prevalent in the high-troponin group (17.1% vs 7.1%), though no formal p-value was calculated due to small expected counts. In ESRD, AF is associated with volume overload, electrolyte disturbances, and structural atrial disease — all of which also promote troponin elevation. The pattern is clinically consistent even if not formally significant here.

No p-value (small counts)
Paced Rhythm

Only 2 patients (one per group) had paced rhythms — far too few to draw any conclusions. Paced rhythm also confounds ECG interpretation (ST segments and QRS morphology are unreliable), so these patients were counted but their ECG morphology data should be interpreted cautiously.

n = 2 — Insufficient for analysis
QTc Interval

Despite the theoretical concern that higher troponin might accompany longer QTc (through myocardial fibrosis affecting repolarisation), there was no significant difference between groups (430 vs 445 ms, p = 0.593). The wide IQRs in the high-troponin group (390–470 ms) suggest high variability, likely due to electrolyte fluctuations inherent to haemodialysis.

p = 0.593 — Not significant
QRS Duration ★ Significant

This was the only ECG interval to differ significantly between groups (94 ms high vs 100 ms low, p = 0.046). Paradoxically, the high-troponin group had a shorter median QRS. The wide IQRs (62–126 ms in high group) suggest high within-group variability. This may reflect a subset of patients with intraventricular conduction defects (LBBB, paced rhythm) skewing the high group's IQR rather than a true biological effect. Sensitivity analyses in Table 3 (rs = −0.22) confirm this borderline finding.

p = 0.046 — Significant ✓ (interpret cautiously)
PQ Interval

Available in 72/83 patients (AF and paced rhythm patients excluded as PQ is unmeasurable). No significant difference between groups (172 vs 163 ms, p = 0.641). PR interval prolongation, which would suggest AV nodal disease, does not track with troponin levels in this cohort.

p = 0.641 — Not significant
ST-Segment Depression (STD)

Present in ~29% of all patients — a high background prevalence reflecting the uraemic cardiomyopathy substrate. Remarkably, STD was equally distributed between high and low troponin groups (26.8% vs 31.0%, p = 0.679). This confirms that chronic troponin elevation in ESRD does not result from ongoing ischaemia detectable by surface ECG.

p = 0.679 — Not significant
T-Wave Inversion (TWI)

T-wave inversion was present in 43.4% of all patients — strikingly high compared to the general population, reflecting widespread ventricular strain and repolarisation abnormalities in ESRD. However, TWI did not differ between troponin groups (46.3% vs 40.5%, p = 0.590), suggesting it is a marker of the ESRD substrate generally, not specifically linked to troponin magnitude.

p = 0.590 — Not significant
LV Strain Pattern

Defined as simultaneous STD + TWI in the lateral leads, LV strain was present in 25.3% overall — a clinically significant proportion. Strikingly, the prevalence was nearly identical between troponin groups (24.4% vs 26.2%, p = 0.850). This dissociation between ECG strain pattern and troponin level reinforces that chronic troponin elevation in ESRD reflects sustained structural damage rather than acute ischaemia.

p = 0.850 — Not significant
Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH)

LVH was present in 47% of all patients by ECG criteria — consistent with the known high prevalence in ESRD driven by pressure overload (hypertension, uremia). Importantly, LVH was not more prevalent in the high-troponin group (43.9% vs 50.0%, p = 0.578). This null finding suggests that LVH per se is not the primary mechanism of chronic troponin elevation in ESRD.

p = 0.578 — Not significant
Pathological Q Waves

Q waves (markers of prior transmural MI) were more common in the high-troponin group (12.2% vs 4.8%) but not significantly so (p = 0.265). Included in the multivariable GLM to test for independent effect — the model confirmed no significant independent contribution (B = −0.385, p = 0.146), even adjusting for age, Kt/V, and diabetes.

p = 0.265 — Not significant
Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB)

LBBB was rare (3 patients total) and equally uncommon in both groups. Too few cases to draw conclusions. LBBB is clinically important as it may cause troponin elevation through mechanical dyssynchrony independent of coronary disease, but this could not be assessed with only 3 cases.

p = 0.830 — Not significant (n too small)
QTc Prolongation (>440 ms)

QTc prolongation affected 28.9% of all patients — a high and clinically concerning prevalence (associated with ventricular arrhythmia risk). However, prevalence was virtually identical between groups (29.3% vs 28.6%, p = 0.944). This striking null finding confirms that chronically elevated troponin and QTc prolongation are independent manifestations of uraemic cardiomyopathy.

p = 0.944 — Not significant

Age — rs = +0.27, p = 0.015

A weak but statistically significant positive correlation: as age increases, so does hs-Troponin I. This is consistent with cumulative myocardial injury over time in ESRD — decades of uraemic toxin exposure, repetitive haemodynamic stress from dialysis, and progressive myocardial fibrosis. The effect persisted independently in the multivariable GLM (p = 0.003), confirming age as the primary driver of troponin elevation in this cohort.

p = 0.015 — Significant ✓ | Confirmed in GLM
Kt/V — rs = −0.34, p = 0.001

The strongest correlation in the dataset: patients with lower dialysis adequacy (lower Kt/V) have higher troponin. rs = −0.34 represents a moderate-strength inverse relationship. Mechanistically, inadequate dialysis leads to uraemic toxin accumulation, which directly damages cardiomyocytes and impairs myocardial function. A 0.1-unit decrease in Kt/V corresponds to approximately 6.5% rise in troponin (e^0.0635 = 1.065). This relationship was attenuated but borderline in the GLM (p = 0.051) after adjusting for age and diabetes.

p = 0.001 — Strongest correlation ✓
Dialysis Duration — rs = +0.12, p = 0.279

Despite the biological rationale (longer ESRD → more cumulative damage → higher troponin), dialysis duration shows no significant correlation with troponin. This may reflect that the rate of myocardial injury matters more than the total duration, or that patients with the most severe disease die earlier, creating a survivor bias that flattens the apparent relationship over time.

p = 0.279 — Not significant
CRP — rs = +0.06, p = 0.593

CRP, a marker of systemic inflammation, showed essentially zero correlation with troponin (rs = 0.06 is negligible). This null finding is clinically important: it argues against systemic inflammation being the primary mechanism of chronic troponin elevation in ESRD, pointing instead to structural, haemodynamic, and uraemic mechanisms as the dominant drivers.

p = 0.593 — Not significant
QRS Duration — rs = −0.22, p = 0.049

A weak inverse correlation that barely crossed the significance threshold. Paradoxically, higher troponin is associated with slightly shorter QRS duration. This likely reflects competing effects: LBBB (causing QRS widening) clustering in some high-troponin patients, while the overall group has more fibrotic, smaller ventricles with faster conduction. This borderline finding should be interpreted cautiously — it was not significant in Table 2 as a dichotomous variable and approaches the threshold only as a continuous correlation.

p = 0.049 — Borderline (interpret with caution)
QTc Interval — rs = +0.06, p = 0.593

No meaningful correlation between QTc and troponin level (rs = 0.06 is negligible). This is notable given theoretical mechanisms by which myocardial fibrosis could prolong repolarisation. The null finding suggests that in ESRD, QTc prolongation is driven predominantly by electrolyte disturbances (hyperkalaemia, hypocalcaemia) rather than by the degree of troponin-associated myocardial injury.

p = 0.593 — Not significant

Hover over any predictor row to understand what the effect estimate means in clinical terms and how to interpret confidence intervals in the context of this model.

Age — B = +0.016, p = 0.003

Age is the only variable reaching independent statistical significance. A B of +0.016 means that for each additional year of age, log(troponin) increases by 0.016 units — translating to approximately a 1.6% increase in troponin per year of age (e^0.016 ≈ 1.016). The 95% CI fully excludes zero (+0.005 to +0.027), confirming this is a robust independent effect. This finding supports the concept that cumulative myocardial injury from ESRD drives troponin elevation progressively with age, independent of other clinical factors.

p = 0.003 — Sole significant independent predictor ✓
Kt/V — B = −0.635, p = 0.051

Kt/V narrowly missed significance after adjusting for age and diabetes (p = 0.051). The 95% CI just touches zero (−1.273 to −0.002), indicating the model estimates a negative effect but with marginal precision. A one-unit increase in Kt/V would correspond to a 47% decrease in troponin (e^−0.635), but a more realistic 0.1-unit increase corresponds to ~6.3% decrease. The attenuation from the univariate level (p = 0.001 in Table 3) to p = 0.051 in the adjusted model is likely due to age confounding — older patients may have lower Kt/V through non-adherence or access issues. With a larger sample, Kt/V would likely achieve independent significance.

p = 0.051 — Borderline; clinically important
Diabetes Mellitus — B = +0.301, p = 0.052

Diabetes mellitus showed a borderline independent association with higher troponin (B = +0.301, p = 0.052). The CI just touches zero (−0.003 to +0.604). A B of +0.301 translates to a 35% higher troponin in diabetic vs non-diabetic patients (e^0.301 ≈ 1.35), adjusting for age, Kt/V, and Q waves. Diabetes in ESRD causes additional microvascular myocardial damage, autonomic neuropathy affecting cardiac function, and accelerated atherosclerosis — all contributing to troponin elevation. The borderline p-value likely reflects insufficient power (n=82, only 41 diabetics).

p = 0.052 — Borderline; likely significant with larger N
Pathological Q Waves — B = −0.385, p = 0.146

Q waves (markers of prior transmural MI) showed no independent association with troponin (p = 0.146). Counterintuitively, the effect estimate is negative (B = −0.385), suggesting patients with Q waves may trend toward slightly lower troponin in the adjusted model — though this is not significant. This is likely a spurious finding from the small number of Q wave cases (n=7). Sensitivity analyses replacing Q waves with LV strain, ST depression, or T-wave inversion all confirmed no significant independent ECG effects, validating the conclusion that surface ECG morphology does not independently predict troponin magnitude in ESRD.

p = 0.146 — Not significant; ECG not an independent predictor
